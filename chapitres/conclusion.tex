\chapter*{Conclusion \& Perspectives \markboth{Conclusion}{}}
\addcontentsline{toc}{chapter}{Conclusion}
\setcounter{section}{0}

Introductory to the conclusion is proposed a summary of the context surrounding our work. Throughout the literature, metaporous structures have shown {efficient acoustic absorption properties} from a combination of the effects of the periodicity, the dispersion of the waves caused by the scatterers, and the attenuation of waves inherent to porous or poroelastic layers. In Chapter 1, we have identified the {existing numerical methods} developed for the computation of the scattering and the dispersion of waves in homogeneous layers and periodic systems. Even though these effects have been studied in the literature, the computation of dispersion relations of waves has been mainly focused on {closed systems} so far.\\

The originality of this work lies in the extension of the considerations to the study of {leaky guided waves in metaporous layers} radiating to surrounding media, thus leading to account for leakage on top of the other dissipation mechanism leading to challenges in the modeling process, due to the combination of losses. In this work, we have identified that the {classification of the types of existing wave solutions} in these configurations was of interest. To this end, methods were developed to {1)} compute the complex wavenumber-real frequency dispersion relations of leaky guided waves propagating in homogeneous multilayer structures, {2)} evaluate the scattering properties of grating stacks and {3)} compute the dispersion relations of leaky guided waves in metaporous layers. \\

The dispersion equations modeling {guided waves in homogeneous layers with leakage} to surrounding air can be derived analytically for simple configurations. However, the introduction of the SCM in the case of multilayer structures has allowed for an {efficient computation} of the modes in these structures. Describing these configurations is done by prescribing a harmonic motion in the infinite direction of the layers while using a {1D discretization} to describe the propagation along the transverse direction. With this general formalism, any stack of elastic, fluid or \acrshort{pem} layer or a combination thereof can be modeled. Results regarding the energy flux for specific modes were presented as well. This method was implemented and validated as a {fast and accurate technique} for reliably computing the dispersion relations. Its efficiency and stability were demonstrated through a comparison with semi-analytical results. 

Results have included the study of a two-layer structure composed of a melamine layer coupled to an aluminium layer for which the frequencies of interest were set to the range where the first orders of the modes of the poroelastic layer were observable. In this regime, the plate could be {assumed to be almost rigid}, leading to weak leakage effects and almost decoupled modes for each part of the structure. The leakage effects have revealed to be much more prominent in the case of a simple poroelastic layer coupled on each side to surrounding media, due in part to the high porosity of the melamine implying a stronger coupling to the fluid. The presence of {important dissipations} in these materials combines with the leakage effects to create complex physical phenomena, that appeared to {vary from the existing classification of leaky wave solutions} in non-dissipative layers proposed in the thesis of Kiefer \cite{kiefer2022}. \\

The approach proposed for the calculation of the {scattering properties of periodic multilayer structures} has consisted of a {plane wave formalism} in order to describe all of the non-periodic domains of the structure (surrounding half-spaces and homogeneous layers added when considering coatings or sandwich structures) that is {coupled to a FE discretization} of the periodic layers. This coupling is imposed by introducing a {state vector formalism} at each interface. This method has allowed to deal with grating stacks constituted of fluid, elastic or poroelastic layers and was shown to {remain stable} for all the tested cases contrary to the TMM as illustrated by the results of this chapter, covering examples of configurations presented in the literature.\\

The assumptions regarding the nature of the {wave solutions in the surrounding medium} within the paradigm of a dispersion problem have been one of the main interest in this work. They have been discussed in the case of homogeneous layers in Section~\ref{sec:homog_layer}. We have obtained from this study that the radiating wave solutions {should not necessarily be outgoing waves}. The proper modes selected have been described in the case of homogeneous layers and used in a similar fashion for periodic structures.

All of these steps have brought to the analysis of the dispersion relation of leaky guided acoustic waves in metaporous surfaces. In a porous unit cell modeled as an equivalent fluid with an embedded rigid inclusion, several phenomenon were analysed. We were able to highlight the {trapped mode} occuring in a case where the cylindrical inclusion is tuned to show {perfect absorption} for a given frequency. The study of split-ring resonators has shown the impact of the resonance due to an {hybrization between the cavity and the mode of the layer}. The existence of {exceptional points} for this kind of configuration was first discussed in Xiong \etal\cite{xiong2017} and obtained for a rather different layout in the present case. \\

This thesis work has proposed the development and validation of several {numerical tools} for the modeling of metaporous surfaces and interfaces. While most of the geometries for which results have been presented in this thesis do not constitute novel configurations, they have been used to {validate new methods and approaches}. The introduction of wave leakage and the investigation of the complex wavenumber-real frequency dispersion relations has also shed a new light onto these configurations.

\section*{Perspectives and future work}
\addcontentsline{toc}{section}{Perspectives}

While the finite elements approach developed and presented in Chapter 3 has been tested and validated for configurations with arbitrary fluid, elastic or poroelastic material nature, the dispersion relations presented in Chapter 4 are only studied for the case of an equivalent fluid. Future work thus includes the generalization of this study to dispersion relations of waves in metaporous structures modeled with poroelastic unit cells and embedded solid inclusions. This extension will lead to analyse a much denser dispersion relation due to the presence of additional sets of modes linked to the elastic phase for materials modeled with the full Biot theory, as was studied for homogeneous layers in Chapter 2 and in a few papers for 2D periodic configurations \cite{wang2020,zhang2023}. The core modeling part of the numerical implementation does not constitute an issue as it was already validated in Chapter 3 to compute scattering properties. However, obtaining proper results for the dispersion relations of periodic poroelastic layers is expected to require minor adjustments to the root-finding numerical framework and require also a thorough analysis of the results. Finally, the in-depth investigation into the behavior of the system could include a more systematic study of the existing energy conversion mechanisms. This could be done by taking advantage of the possibility to calculate the energy transport velocity and energy flux for all solved modes.\\

An important perspective for the calculations of dispersion relations would involve the development of a more robust method, breaking away from the currently used root-finding procedure. Starting from the presentation of the method covered in Section~\ref{sec:method} from Chapter 4, time was spent trying to write the equations in the form of an eigenvalue problem with the Bloch wavenumbers $k_{1q}$ as parameters. Difficulties have been met in obtaining such a formulation, due to the way the coupling between the surrounding medium and the structure is enforced. The finite elements matrices obtained in Section~\ref{coupling-models} depend on both a term in $e^{ik_{1q}d}$ (due to the periodicity) and an integral on $x_1$ with a $ e^{ik_{1q}}x_1 $ term (due to the wave radiation), which presents itself as a very unfriendly form to arrive at the expression of an eigenvalue problem. However, an appropriate change of variable or a different formulation for the coupling might provide a way to achieve this. Note that we have stratched the surface of this topic as briefly discussed in Section.~\ref{app:neumann_bc}, showing that an eigenvalue problem is easily written in the case of a closed system where the coupling to the surrounding medium does not exist and is replaced by Neumann-type boundary conditions.

Another approach would be to further investigate methods akin to contour integral evaluation in order to search for roots, with e.g.the Beyn method \cite{guttel2017, liu2025}. Although these iterative procedures have the advantage of not requiring initial guesses to construct an interpolation, the contour integrals should still be computed in the vicinity of a given central value, thus still requiring some sort of initialization. Additionally, the quality of the matrix conditioning should be explored as well due to the amount of numerical operations required by these methods (e.g. the successive SVD computations involved should be computed so as to have sufficient precision for singular values). \\

Prospects for this work are opening up in terms of experimental investigation on these configurations. As the thesis focused essentially on numerics, only limited experimental results have been shown in Section~\ref{sec:expe} with the two-layer configuration. Some design of a more appropriate setup could be worked on with the now available numerical tools, and finding the appropriate materials and geometry to use could be of interest in order to be able to experimentally observe dissipative waves that show significant leakage.

A study into the excitability of the modes could be conducted by using a numerical model done with Comsol Multiphysics. In this case, the model would require a fully discretized surrounding fluid domain the use of a properly tuned line source to excite the array of scatterer. The tuning should done by taking the proper wavenumber and frequency characterizing so that a given is properly excited. This setup could be used to confirm the form of solutions that were found in this work for modes in a more realistic and finite structures, leading to further link the dispersion relation results to an actual observable behaviour of the structures. This seems as an important aspect as many dissipation mechanisms involved lead to attenuated and numerous evanescent modes contributing to the wave field. This preliminary study of mode excitability for these systems will also help the design of an experimental setup. Questions to be raised also concern the possible restrictions of contributions of leaky modes to the near field only \cite{sapozhnikov2007,nedospasov2017,abbaszadeh2024}.

Simulation results obtained in Chapter 4 could allow for further experimental validations for the presented cases. A simple example could be a melamine porous sample manufactured with embedded rigid (steel) cylindrical inclusions. Challenges include the coupling between the scatterers and the host layer but most importantly, the precision required to engineer precisely the radius of the cylinders since dramatic changes have been observed on the dispersion relation with even very small (the order of a tenth of a millimetre) variations of radius. Measurements need to take into account the nature of the source needed to excite the variety of these modes, as was briefly discussed in Section~\ref{app:slatcow}. A post-process of the frequency responses using SLaTCoW \cite{geslain2016} or some other method allowing for the retrieval of complex wavenumbers should be therefore put in place.
