\chapter*{Conclusion \& Perspectives \markboth{Conclusion}{}}
\addcontentsline{toc}{chapter}{Conclusion}
\setcounter{section}{0}

This thesis work has proposed the development and validation of several numerical tools allowing to model metaporous surfaces and interfaces. These structures feature dissipation mechanisms, strong dispersion due to the periodicity as well as leakage, thus involving challenges in the modeling process. Methods were developed to compute the dispersion relations of leaky guided waves propagating in homogeneous multilayer structures, evaluate the scattering properties of grating stacks and compute dispersion relations of waves in metaporous layers.

Throughout the literature, these metaporous structures have shown efficient absorption properties using various geometry by embedding resonant, tortuous paths, voids or inclusions inside porous and poroelastic layers. These obstacles provide additional dispersion as well as interferences of the wave at discrete frequencies. Combined with the attenuation of waves in porous layer, they provide for efficient acoustic absorption structures. Even though, these effects have been studied in the literature, they have mostly been considered as infinite and closed systems. In this work, the considerations were extended to the study of leaky guided waves radiating to surrounding media. The combination of these loss mechanisms thus have showned a complex behaviour featured by the structure and that the classification of the waves was of interest. From a literature overview and a presentation of the topics surrounding this thesis work, we have identified the various exisitng numerical methods allowing for the computation of the scattering properties and the dispersion of waves in homogeneous layers and periodic systems. 

The complex behaviour of these structures was not a major issue when considering the computation of the scattering properties of these configurations, since the surrounding media are inherently considered in the approach. The approach has consisted of a plane wave formalism in order to describe all of the non-periodic elements in the structure (surrounding half-spaces and homogeneous layers when considering coatings or sandwich structures) that is coupled to a FE discretization of the periodic layers. This coupling is imposed by introducing a state vector formalism at each interface. This robust and stable method allows to deal with grating stacks constituted of fluid, elastic or poroelastic layers. The FE discretization has shown to remain stable for all the tested cases contrary to the TMM.

The modeling of the dispersion of guided waves in homogeneous layers with leakage to surrounding air can be derived analytically in simple configurations. However, the introduction of the SCM in the case of multilayer structures has allowed for an efficient computation of the modes in these structures. The description of these configurations is done by prescribing a harmonic motion in the infinite direction of the layers while using a 1D discretization to describe the propagation along the transverse direction. With this general formalism, any stack of layer, dissipative or not, can be presented. Results regarding the energy flux for specific modes were presented as well. This method was implemented and validated as a fast and accurate technique for reliably computing the dispersion relations. Its efficiency and stability were demonstrated through comparisons with semi-analytical results. Results have included the study of a two-layer structure.

In the framework of Chapter 3, the two-layer system that was studied was composed of a melamine layer coupled to an aluminium layer. In the frequency range of interest where the first modes of the poroelastic layer were highlighted, the plate could be assumed to be almost rigid, leading to weak leakage effects. These have revealed to be much more prominent in the case of a simple poroelastic layer coupled on each side to surrounding media. The presence of important dissipation mechanisms in these materials combines with the leakage effects to create complex physical phenomena, that appeared to vary from the classification of the existing leaky wave solutions in non-dissipative layers \cite{kiefer2022}. The assumptions regarding the plane wave solutions that exist in the surrounding medium have thus been one of the main interest in this work and have been discussed for homogeneous layers in Section~\ref{sec:homog_layer}. We have obtained from this study that the radiating wave solutions should not necessarily be outgoing solutions. The proper modes selected have been described in the case of homogeneous layers and used in a similar fashion for metaporous surfaces. 

All of these steps have brought to the analysis of the dispersion relation of leaky guided acoustic waves in metaporous surfaces. The configuration is a porous unit cell modeled as an equivalent fluid with embedded rigid inclusion. We were able to highlight the trapped mode that occurs in a case where the cylindrical inclusion is tuned to show perfect absorption for a given frequency. The study of split-ring resonators has shown the impact of the resonance due to an hybrization between the cavity and the modes of the layer. The existence of exceptional points for this kind of configuration was first discussed in Xiong \etal\cite{xiong2017} and obtained in a rather different layout in the present case. 

This work thesis has focused mainly on the development of numerical tools rather than new configurations exhibiting exotic acoustic properties and has shown dispersion relation for metaporous layers accounting for the wave leakage. Examples from the literature whether they were homogeneous stack of layers or porous layers with macroscopic inclusion were revisited to study more in-depth their modal behaviour. 

\subsection*{Perspectives and future work}

\textbf{1.} The finite elements approach developed and presented in Chapter 3 has been tested and validated for configurations with arbitrary fluid, elastic or poroelastic material nature. However, dispersion relations presented in Chapter 4 are only studied for the case of the equivalent fluid. Future work thus includes an generalization of this study on the dispersion relations of waves in metaporous structures, by assuming poroelastic layers as well as solid inclusions. This extension leads to the analysis of a much denser dispersion relation due to the presence of additional sets of modes linked to the elastic phase of the materials modeled with the full Biot theory, as was studied for homogeneous layers in Chapter 2 and in a few papers for 2D periodic configurations \cite{wang2020,zhang2023}. While the numerical implementation does not consistitute an issue as the core modeling part has been already validated in Chapter 3 to compute scattering properties, the analysis of the results of the dispersion relations of periodic poroelastic unit cell with scatterers is expected to require minor adjustments to the numerical framework and require also some thorough analysis of the results. Finally, the in-depth investigation into the behavior of the system could include a more systematic study of the energy conversion mechanisms that exist. This could be done by taking more advantages of the possibility of computing the energy velocities for each mode and the energy flows.

\textbf{2.} An important perspective for the metaporous structure dispersion relation computation presented in Chapter 4 would involve the development of a more robust method that could break away from the currently root-finding procedure that is used. By looking at the presentation of the method covered in Section~\ref{sec:method} from Chapter 4, time has been spent during the development of the method to try to write the equations in the form of an eigenvalue problem with $k_{1q}$ as the eigenvalue to be solved. There exists some challenges for obtaining such a formulation with the way the coupling between the surrounding medium and the structure is done. The finite elements matrices obtained in Section~\ref{coupling-models} depend on both on a term in $ e^{ik_{1q}d} $ and an integral on $x_1$  with a $ e^{ik_{1q}}x_1 $ term, which presents itself as a quite unfriendly form to obtain an eigenvalue problem. An appropriate change of variable or a different formulation for the coupling might provide a way to achieve the writing of an eigenvalue problem. Note that we have stratched the surface of this topic as briefly discussed in Section.~\ref{app:neumann_bc}, showing that the eigenvalue problem is easily written when the coupling to the surrounding medium does not exist and is replaced by Neumann-type boundary condition. 

Perspectives on this could include: methods like contour integral evaluation in order to search for the root by using the Beyn method for example \cite{guttel2017, liu2025}. Although these iterative procedures have the advantage of not requiring initial guesses, the contour integrals should still be computed around a given value, thus still requiring some sort of initialization. Additionally, the quality of the matrix conditioning should be investigated as well due to the amount of numerical operations required by these methods (e.g. the successive SVD computations involved should be computed so as to have sufficient precision for singular values). 

\textbf{3.} A study the excitability of the modes could be conducted by using a numerical model done with COMSOL \& a line source excitation properly tuned according to the dispersion relation modes to confirm the form of the solutions of the modes in finite structures and a more \emph{realistic} configuration and further link the dispersion relation results to an actual observable behaviour of the structures by gaining insights on the excitability of the modes. This is an important aspect as there are many dissipation mechanisms involved, leading to very attenuated modes: seeing if they can be excited or if their existence actually affects the wave propagation in any way seems as an interesting direction for this project. This preliminary study should help the design of an experimental setup. Questions to be raised also concern the possible restrictions of contributions from leaky modes to the near field\cite{sapozhnikov2007,nedospasov2017,abbaszadeh2024} \comment{citer thèse de liu \cite{liu2017} ?}.

Simulation results in these situations could allow for further experimental validations for the configurations of interest. A simple example could be a melamine porous sample manufactured with embedded rigid (steel) cylindrical inclusions. Challenges include the coupling between the scatterers and the host layer but most importantly, the precision required to engineer precisely the radius of the cylinders since dramatic changes have been observed on the dispersion relation with small variations of radius (of the order of 0.1 mm). Measurements need to take into account the nature of the source needed to excite the variety of these modes, as was briefly discussed in Section~\ref{app:slatcow}. A post-process of the frequency responses using SLaTCoW \cite{geslain2016} or some other method allowing for the retrieval of complex wavenumbers should be therefore put in place.
